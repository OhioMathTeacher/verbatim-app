The reply rules shared by all three drafts, and why they are there.

Measured in the May corpus once speakers could be told apart: students took 45%
of the turns and wrote 7% of the words. The model produced 148 words for every
14 of theirs. The old prompt asked once for short replies and was ignored.

  - 60-word cap, stated as a rule and repeated at the end
  - one question per reply, then stop
  - never answer your own question
  - never supply a value the student could produce
  - no headings, bold or bullets, which inflate reading load
  - mathematics as TeX between dollars, which the page draws. Asked for because
    the May corpus is full of replies like `x^3x^3x^3` and pipe-drawn tables:
    the model reaches for formatting whether or not anything renders it, and
    naming the one form that does is cheaper than forbidding the rest
  - no personality questions at all (29 Oct 2026). Two replaced the original five
    after the May transcripts showed that test consuming roughly a third of a
    twenty-minute activity before any mathematics; with three sessions a student
    would still answer the same two three times. How the AI sounds is now set in
    the page (--voice), recorded on every AI turn, and not part of the prompt
