🔑 Getting an AI key

The activities don't come with an AI attached. You point one at an AI service of your choosing, using a key — a long string of letters and numbers that tells the service the requests are yours. Getting one takes about three minutes and doesn't require knowing anything about programming.

Why a key, rather than just a login

The chat website you may already use (Gemini, ChatGPT, Claude and so on) is a finished product. The activities here are a different page talking to the same underlying model directly, and a key is how that page identifies itself. It is the ordinary way this is done; it is not a developer-only thing.

Practically, it also means the conversation is yours. It isn't routed through anyone else's server, and no account of ours is involved.

Gemini, step by step

  1. Go to aistudio.google.com/apikey and sign in with a Google account. A personal Gmail account works best; some school accounts have this turned off.
  2. Press Create API key. If it asks for a project, let it make one for you.
  3. Copy the key. If you lose it, no harm done: come back to the same page and copy it again, or make another.
  4. Open any activity, press AI Setup, choose the $0 tab, then Gemini, and paste the key in. It turns into dots once saved.

Your key stays in your browser. It is not written into the activity page, not sent to us, and not stored anywhere but on your own device. If you clear your browser data it will be gone and you'll simply paste it again.

What it costs

Nothing. Gemini's free tier needs no card, and a twenty-minute conversation of this kind is far inside its limits. The free tier does cap how many requests a key can make per minute, so for a whole class it is better for each student to use their own key than to share one.

If Gemini is awkward

Groq, also under $0, is free with any email address — no Google account needed. Under Pay (Own Key) are Claude, OpenAI and OpenRouter, billed to your own account. Every one works the same way: sign up, make a key, paste it in. The activity itself doesn't care which you choose, though for a class it's simpler if everyone uses the same one.
